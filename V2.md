# Compass v2 — Court Screens

v1 is untouched. `/display`, `/scorer`, `/control` and everything under them behave exactly as
before. v2 lives entirely under `/v2`, `/api/v2` and `src/lib/v2`, and adds two new database
tables. The two versions can be used side by side, or v1 can simply be ignored.

## One-time setup

The v2 screens store their state in Postgres (not process memory) so a coach's phone and the
TV on the other side of the court always agree. Create the two new tables once:

```bash
npx prisma db push
```

Both tables are new — nothing in the existing tournament data is altered. Until this runs, the
v2 pages say so plainly instead of half-working.

## The screens

| URL | Who opens it |
| --- | --- |
| `/v2` | Hub — links to every screen, one row per court |
| `/v2/tv/2` | The TV **at court 2**. Open `/v2/tv/3` on court 3's TV, and so on |
| `/v2/coach` | A coach picks their court once; the phone remembers it |
| `/v2/coach/2` | The coach console for court 2 |
| `/v2/ceremony` | The announcer's remote for the awards presentation |

## What a court TV shows

The screen for court 2 only ever talks about court 2 — except during the awards, when every
screen in the venue joins the same presentation.

1. **Between matches** — the next match on this court across the top, standings underneath.
2. **Live** — the coach taps *Start match*, and the screen becomes a full-screen scoreboard:
   names, sets, games and the current point, plus GAME! / SET! beats as they happen. Scoring a
   point also takes the screen live, so a coach who forgets the button loses nothing.
3. **Winner** — the moment the match ends the screen builds before it pays off. For about two
   and a half seconds it says only *"And the winner is…"* with a spinning ball and a filling
   bar — long enough for everyone to look up — and then the name lands full screen with a white
   flash, confetti and rotating light rays. The championship final gets the cup instead of a
   medal. **There is no timer.** It stays until that court's coach taps *Finish*.
4. **Waiting for results** — once every match in the tournament is played, the screens hold a
   "results presentation coming up" card. Standings are deliberately hidden so the placings are
   still a surprise.
5. **Ceremony** — see below.
6. **Final standings** — after the awards have been given out the screens rest on the medal
   table: the cup and the champion's name across the top, gold, silver and bronze rows below.
   This is the screen players photograph, so it is laid out as a keepsake rather than a table.

## The awards presentation

From `/v2/ceremony`:

1. **Choose who gets announced.** Every place on the podium is a toggle. No third-place medals
   today? Turn third place off and the presentation runs second → first. The reveal order is
   always deepest place first.
2. **Announce results.** Every court TV switches to a holding card ("players to centre court").
   The podium is frozen at this moment, so a late score correction cannot reshuffle names
   mid-presentation.
3. **Reveal one place per tap**, and each reveal is two beats. First the call — *"third place
   goes to…"* with the medal and a filling bar, the podium still showing a question mark — then
   the name lands full screen. The champion gets a longer build and the cup. After a reveal
   nothing moves until the organiser taps again, so the medal, the handshake and the photo all
   happen before the next name appears. *Step back* undoes a reveal; *Peek* shows the next name
   on the phone only.
4. **Close** returns every screen to the final standings.

**Music.** The court screens play their own ceremony music, synthesised in the browser (no audio
files to fail to load): a holding chord while the players gather, a rising build under each call,
a hit when a name lands, a full fanfare for the champion, and a loop under the final podium. The
switch is on the announcer's remote, not on the screens — they are the one person who knows
whether the hall PA is already playing. Browsers will not start audio on their own, so the first
time a ceremony runs each TV shows a small *"Tap for sound"* button; one tap per screen, once.

## How the state machine works

Only one bit per court is stored (`idle` / `live`) plus which match is on air. Everything else
is derived from the tournament data in `src/lib/v2/stage.ts`:

- live + the active match finished → **winner** screen
- ceremony running → **ceremony** on every court
- all matches played → **waiting**

That is deliberate. A stored "winner" flag could disagree with reality after an undo or a score
correction, and a TV stuck celebrating an un-completed match is the worst kind of bug on a
screen nobody can reach.

## Notes

- The coach console reuses v1's scoring API, so scores entered in v2 appear in v1's display and
  results pages, and vice versa. *Edit score* opens v1's full score editor for the rare case
  where a result needs rebuilding set by set.
- Coaches authenticate with the same tournament PIN as v1.
- Podium ranking: round-robin uses the final table; a compass draw uses the East final for first
  and second and the West (consolation) final for third.
